# Foo Fighters, Shinedown and Alter Bridge: recording research

Research date: 2026-10-01. Targets are the original album versions: Everlong
and My Hero (*The Colour and the Shape*, 1997), The Pretender (*Echoes, Silence,
Patience & Grace*, 2007), Walk (*Wasting Light*, 2011), Sound of Madness
(*The Sound of Madness*, 2008), Cut the Cord (*Threat to Survival*, 2015),
Monsters and Devil (*Attention Attention*, 2018), and Blackbird (*Blackbird*,
2007). “Evelong” is interpreted as **Everlong**.

## Evidence contract

Artist/engineer statements establish historical context; a session inventory
does not establish the rig for every overdub. Lessons/transcriptions establish
playable arrangements with varying confidence. Every numerical app setting is
a design proposal. We have not listened to the recordings or extracted stems;
album matching and pickup calibration remain pending.

GuitarRack's amps are original approximations and its cabinets are synthetic
filters. They are not measured responses of the named amps, speakers, mics or
rooms. None of the proposed settings are authenticated hardware knob positions.
Only existing processors are used. Physically tune the guitar: no spectral
pitch is inserted, and all templates load in Legacy 128. Independent doubles,
countermelodies, bass, strings and keyboards require independent performances
or instruments. More gain does not recreate them.

## Foo Fighters: three recording eras

### Everlong and My Hero

Bradley Cook's original Produce Like A Pro interviews describe the 1997
sessions and overdub workflow [F1]. Huart's later Cook-based My Hero feature
describes complementary Fender/Mesa amp paths, SM57/U87 capture, and balancing
their contributions with a polarity cancellation check before restoring normal
polarity [F2]. This supports a complementary amp-family blend and disciplined
part construction, not phase inversion as a permanent effect.

An important naming trap: the 2025 text calls the Fender a “Tone Master Twin
Reverb.” That wording does not authenticate the later digital Tone Master
Twin product as 1997 equipment. Here the evidence is retained at **Fender
Tone Master / Mesa family**, with model naming unresolved. Generic web recipes
also conflate album-wide Rectifier usage with a verified Everlong-only rig.
Specific Mesa revision, cabinet, pickup, per-overdub balance and knob positions
are not recovered. The widely circulated Gibson RD claim points to an embedded
Cook video; its audio was not independently reviewed here, so no exact RD
assignment is required by these racks.

**Proposed Everlong roles:** ringing intro crunch, fuller chord rhythm,
upper-part detail and a cleaner breakdown alternative. The album's opening
electric part is not treated as the familiar solo acoustic performance. Keep
add-nine chord separation, low-D attack and pick dynamics; excessive saturation
blurs the inner notes. Clean Breakdown is a reduced-drive application alternative,
not a newly verified clean amp track. [T1]

**Proposed My Hero roles:** body-and-edge rhythm, octave/melody detail and
reduced-drive breakdown. The main rhythm can use the same family blend with
different filtering; the upper voice needs less low end and enough mids to stay
audible. Carl Brown's lesson explicitly covers the differing guitar parts [T3].
Do not insert chorus, E-Bow synthesis or long delays merely to manufacture width.

### The Pretender

Mixer Rich Costey's original Sound On Sound account describes the dense
countermelodies, dynamics and a pair of 1176s on Grohl's guitars, used for
aggression with little actual compression [F3]. The main page could not be
opened; the publisher's indexed interview text was available. It does not
establish a particular amp model. Carl Brown's lesson distinguishes quiet
arpeggios, simultaneous verse parts, open chorus strumming and a separate
bridge riff [T4]. A named teacher's original tutorial specifies standard E
[T4b]; generic “Grohl always uses Drop D” lists conflict and are rejected.

**Proposed roles:** dark clean intro, dry staccato rhythm, open chorus rhythm
and cutting countermelody. The chorus is fuller through voicing and performance,
not a huge gain jump. Do not mistake vocal effects or orchestration for guitar
pedals. A light post-cab compressor in the staccato rack is an application mix
approximation, not an 1176 emulation or recovered ratio.

### Walk

The guitarists' Wasting Light interview is unusually useful [F4]. Grohl names
his Trini Lopez guitars and Fender Tone Master amps; Shiflett names handwired
AC30s, a Marshall JMP and Audio Kitchen amps. Smear describes recording most
of his parts on a modified Hagström/Allparts-neck baritone, through a Peavey
6505 or JC-120 with an MG Lexotone. These are **album-level** statements;
the opening touring-rig description is not used as studio proof. Smear and
Grohl explain how the baritone occupies a complementary register rather than
simply duplicating an ordinary guitar at higher gain.

Vig's Sound On Sound session interview further supports performance-first
three-guitar arrangement and the AC30/Marshall/Audio Kitchen inventory [F5,
publisher search-index text only]. Exact Walk assignments and baritone tuning
were not established. Main-song standard E is supported by the original
Planet Guitar arrangement [T2], not by assuming all three instruments had the
same open-string pitches.

**Proposed roles:** picked intro clean, driving rhythm, upper chime support
and a focused melodic accent/lead. They cover a practical ordinary-guitar
arrangement. No automatically pitch-shifted “Pat baritone” is added. A real
baritone part would need its own verified arrangement and physical tuning;
bass EQ on a standard guitar cannot supply those missing notes.

## Shinedown: 2008, 2015 and 2018 are different targets

### Sound of Madness

Premier Guitar's direct 2022 rundown notes both Myers' album credit and Nick
Perri's lead-guitar credit for The Sound of Madness [S1]. Consequently the
song's studio lead is not automatically labeled “Zach's exact rig.” It is a
later touring source, not a 2008 studio chain. Myers' retrospective interview
mentions discovering Whammy colors during that album's work [S4], but does not
identify exact settings for this song's particular solo. The same interview's
modern JCM800/Kemper/other-amp inventory is not projected backward onto 2008.

**Proposed roles:** tight low-D riff, broader chorus chords and a filtered
lead adaptation. Exact rhythm amp, guitar, overdub and Whammy assignment remain
unverified. The lead uses a restrained fixed wah/EQ color; it cannot recreate
Whammy transposition or its moving overtones. The app's spectral pitch has a
different algorithm and much higher Creative-profile latency, so it is absent.
Physical Drop D is supported by an original teacher's full-song lesson [T5],
with the description available through search; direct video playback failed.

### Cut the Cord

The period Myers interview gives a materially different studio picture [S2]:
Threat to Survival used his '68 ES-335 extensively, selected PRSs/Teles/Strats,
small Rickenbacker and Ampeg combos, a Superfuzz experiment, a JTM45-like custom
head, Orange and tweed options. He describes using fewer tracks and getting
weight from small amps, rather than the previous many-head layering approach.
This does **not** prove which combination was used on every Cut the Cord take.
The artist's own Guitar World playthrough is the primary performance reference
[S3]; only its page/description was accessible, not the moving picture/audio.

**Proposed roles:** rough fuzz/combo riff, more open chorus and filtered hook
accent. An original sustain-fuzz/US-combo route is a playable family adaptation:
it is not a Superfuzz circuit or octave-fuzz reproduction. The nearest bundled
open 2x12 is not the historical eight-inch combo. Drop D follows the published
guitar arrangement [T7], not a caption establishing studio tuning. Make quick
double-stop articulation and accurate stops part of the adjustment process.

### Monsters and Devil

Myers' 2018 interview explicitly places Eric Bass in the producer's chair at
his Charleston studio and emphasizes carefully designed tones [S5]. Its
Fractal XL+/PRS discussion answers a **live setup** question: it does not
authenticate the studio patches. The 2022 rundown's Silver Sky for Monsters,
Axe-Fx III, Fryette power amp, stereo IR/iso-cab/UA OX system is also later live
evidence [S1]. Do not turn that into an exact 2018 preset or cabinet capture.

Myers directly says **Monsters is D standard** in the guitarguitar interview
[S6]. Jeff Perrin's published score agrees [T6]. The PG passage about string
gauges for standard-tuned guitars is generic; its proximity to the Silver Sky
paragraph does not make Monsters standard E. This resolves an incorrect
inference found in generic tone pages. Use D G C F A D, not Drop D.

**Proposed Monsters roles:** verse clarity alternative, wiry main riff,
fuller chorus and melodic accents. A clear/single-coil-compatible edge voice
is informed by live usage, explicitly without a studio pickup claim. Standard
humbuckers can use less bass and input trim. **Devil roles:** dry stop rhythm,
open chorus and a filtered texture adaptation. No synth, vocal or produced
intro timeline is generated. Devil's physical Drop D comes from an original
cover/transcription [T8], with studio confirmation still pending. Generic
pages calling Devil a 2003 Leave a Whisper track are excluded.

## Alter Bridge: Blackbird's two players and two solos

Tremonti's contemporaneous November 2007 interview establishes the album's
Rectifier/Uberschall rhythm pairing and Ecstasy 101B studio leads; he explicitly
distinguishes his live Shiva [A1]. He also describes complementary EQ and lower
gain for tighter, larger recorded guitars. His historical knob examples are
not translated numerically into this app's different dB/percent controls.
The two-model rhythm proposal has shared EQ and one downstream synthetic cab,
so it cannot reproduce two independently miked historical rigs.

The same interview names Kennedy as the bluesier **first** soloist and Tremonti
as the **second** when the heavier backing returns [A1]. The original interview
retrospective describes thumb/two-finger picking, different player approaches,
Kennedy's album Herbert/Mark IV context and Tremonti's Ecstasy [A2]. A later
direct gear interview distinguishes Kennedy's **Modern Eagle solo guitar**
from his general McCarty workhorse [A3, indexed text; full fetch failed]. Do not
replace 2007 equipment with their recent PRS signature heads, Dumbles or 5150s.

**Five proposed roles:** fingerpicked clean, Tremonti body rhythm, Kennedy
supporting rhythm, Kennedy expressive lead and Tremonti articulate lead. The
Kennedy lead has a warmer Mark-family sustain and longer quiet repeats; the
Tremonti lead uses brighter hot-rod-family attack, less low end and shorter
repeats. These are separate playable rack designs, not recovered solo settings.
No always-on wah, chorus or harmony generator is asserted.

**Tuning qualification:** Carl Brown explicitly teaches an Eb-standard
arrangement covering both players [T9]. Community player-by-player lists report
Kennedy in Drop C#/Db and Tremonti in Eb [U1], but are not a firsthand studio
tuning record. The app offers Eb standard for the unified playable arrangement,
with the alternate Kennedy report visible in his metadata; do not call all
studio guitars independently verified in the same tuning. Both lead voices
can be played with that Eb arrangement. If following a Kennedy-specific Drop
C# tab, physically change to C# G# C# F# A# D# and use its fingerings.

## Adjustment and reference-listening pass

Start dry peaks around -18 to -9 dBFS, choose the physical tuning and intended
part, then balance input trim for the pickup. Adjust gain before increasing
master/output: chord attack should remain clear. Compare one guitar role at a
time against the album and avoid matching the full mix's bass weight. For Foo
Fighters, start with moderate gain and short room; for Cut the Cord, retain
rough fuzz/combo articulation; for Monsters, retain D-standard string response;
for Blackbird, match finger dynamics and the two solos separately.

Use an independently played second take to test width. Echo/reverb levels are
conservative adjustable guesses, not measured decay times. The 48-kHz synthetic
phrase headroom audit checks numerical safety, not perceived level, EQ matching,
mic realism or reference tone acceptance. Record pickup, dry level, reference
edition and adjustments when listening; keep catalog approval pending until then.
