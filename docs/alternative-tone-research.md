# Green Day, Soundgarden, Nirvana and System of a Down: recording research

Research date: 2026-10-01. Target: original album recordings, except **The Man
Who Sold the World**, explicitly the **MTV Unplugged in New York** performance.
**Soldier Side** means the full Hypnotize track, not Mezmerize's Soldier Side
(Intro). These are independent playable sounds, not an automated song timeline.

## Evidence and limits

Historical findings below come from named musicians, engineers and published
instructional transcriptions. An album-session inventory is not proof that an
individual instrument was used on every song. Exact studio knob positions,
individual overdub assignments and mic blends usually remain undocumented.
Every numeric GuitarRack control is a proposed application setting, not a
recovered studio recipe. Actual reference listening and pickup calibration are
pending; this work has not listened to or extracted stems from the records.

The application's eight amplifier networks are original approximations. Its
bundled cabinet WAVs are original **synthetic filters**, not authenticated
Marshall, Fender, Mesa, Park, Divided by 13, speaker or microphone responses.
There is no Small Clone, SansAmp, DS-1/DS-2 or Big Muff circuit emulation claim.
Research supports choosing a processor family and signal behavior, not relabeling
that processor as the historical equipment.

The source register provides stable URLs and claim scope. Published first-person
recollections sometimes conflict. They are identified rather than averaged into
an invented equipment list. Commercial release, original mix, remaster and live
performances are distinct references. Do not silently substitute a live rig.

## Recording evidence by era

### Green Day

**Revolution Radio / Outlaws:** Armstrong describes a '56 Les Paul Junior through
a rebuilt Park, a Rickenbacker 360 through a Divided by 13, and a Les Paul through
a Marshall for heavier layers. He describes triple/quadruple tracking, and says
his blue Fernandes did not appear on this album. Engineer Chris Dugan explicitly
identifies bowed guitar on Outlaws. Acoustic parts were generally ribbon-miked;
lead guitar used ribbon/condenser close microphones, with room signals for some
overdubs. These are album-level combinations, not an exact Outlaws or title-track
track sheet. [G1]

**American Idiot / Wake Me Up When September Ends:** the artist interview names
Les Paul Junior/Standard guitars, a Marshall modified with an extra gain stage,
and a modified Park 75 as principal electric equipment. It does not identify
every layer of September Ends or establish that the later Revolution Radio
combination was used here. [G2] The picked opening and heavy electric arrangement
need separate input/drive behavior; Justin Sandercoe's lesson supports the
standard-tuned picking vocabulary. [T3]

### Soundgarden: Badmotorfinger

Thayil identifies the Guild S-100 and explains that he and Cornell offset each
other's guitar colors, sometimes contrasting humbuckers and single coils. His
retrospective allows Peavey, Music Man and Mesa equipment, without identifying a
specific head on each requested song. He describes sometimes reducing treble
drastically; a modern sharp, scooped metal preset is not the only plausible
direction. Outshined has a Thayil solo distinct from Cornell's composition/rhythm
role. [S1] Thayil's published Slaves and Bulldozers recollection describes blowing
on the strings, a performance excitation that more amp gain cannot synthesize.
[S2] The Cornell/Thayil labels below describe intended complementary roles;
exact recorded pickup, amp and pan assignments remain unverified.

### Nirvana: three different recording contexts

**Nevermind:** Vig's session accounts identify Mesa/Boogie, Fender Bassman and Vox
AC30 equipment, Small Clone chorus, and Big Muff for Lithium's darker distortion.
[N1] A later interview specifically recalls RAT/Mesa for Teen Spirit's heavy
sound and AC30/Small Clone for its quieter overdubs. [N2] This is more specific
than generic DS-1-era rig summaries, but it does not settle every guitar layer.
We therefore do not label a proposed hard-clipping voice as a verified DS-1
or RAT recipe. Double tracking and mix decisions contribute to the width.

Vig directly identifies Small Clone as central to Come as You Are's watery
sound. [N3] The manufacturer describes a rate control and two-position depth
switch, not stereo width, multiple chorus voices or tempo synchronization. [N4]
For a close starting behavior, use a **mono, substantial wet/dry blend** with
audible pitch motion, rather than a tiny stereo chorus embellishment. GuitarRack's
existing chorus uses a sinusoidally modulated **12-ms delay with up to ±8 ms**;
it has no BBD model, Small Clone depth switch, feedback or adjustable base delay.
Settings such as 0.75 Hz / 80% depth / 50% mix are hypotheses to test by ear,
not pedal positions converted into measurements. Chorus remains before the amp;
distorted variants put hard distortion before chorus. No chorus is automatically
added to every Nevermind clean/rhythm track.

**In Utero / Heart-Shaped Box:** Albini's 2024 interview describes a Fender Quad
Reverb with internal speakers, an uncertain-model Randall, preferred SansAmp
drive and a rejected-sounding touring Mesa/power-amp rig. He discusses Pedal X,
which crosses tremolo/ring modulation with overdrive, for an experimental
Heart-Shaped Box solo. This is session evidence, not proof that that exact
experimental sound survived the commercial release. [N5] The main templates
target a restrained clean, abrasive rhythm and focused melodic lead; they do
not automatically substitute Pedal X, or declare a Polychorus mandatory.
Exact commercial-solo modulation, guitar model and original-versus-remixed
release differences remain listening/research uncertainties. Online tonewood
reconstruction hypotheses are not verified recording findings.

**MTV Unplugged / The Man Who Sold the World:** Bailey identifies the Martin
D-18E with an added Bartolini 3AV; producer Alex Coletti confirms Cobain used a
Twin Reverb and effects and specifically discusses the amplified sound on this
song. [N6] The related equipment account includes Small Clone and DS-2. [N1]
This does not establish the FOH direct/microphone blend or every pedal switch
state. Pat Smear plays a second acoustic part, not an effect-generated double.
[T8] The electro-acoustic source and lightly colored lead are different targets
from a distorted electric Nevermind rack. A clean room/EQ route deliberately
omits an electric cabinet for an already acoustic/miked source; the colored
route offers restrained drive, mono chorus and clean-combo coloration.
An electric-neck adaptation is explicitly labeled and cannot recreate a Martin
body, acoustic transients, stage spill or Pat's performance.

### System of a Down: Toxicity versus Mezmerize/Hypnotize

**Toxicity / Chop Suey! / Toxicity:** engineer David Schiffman describes Marshall
and Mesa heads/cabinets used together, Mesa for bite and Marshall for body.
SM57/U87 signals were phase-aligned and compressed; he describes Malakian as
preferring minimal effects pedals. [D1] Exact head variants, song-by-song
guitars, cabinet speakers, EQ and gain positions are not established here.
This supports amp-driven attack and controlled low end, not an automatically
inserted Metal Zone or enormous scooped gain. A parallel original Recto/British
blend is useful, but it shares one downstream synthetic cab/EQ and cannot
reproduce two separately miked cabinets or independent overdubs.

**Hypnotize / Soldier Side:** the 2005 artist interview identifies a '62 SG,
JMP100 with stock/extra-gain channels and one 4x12; humbucker parts were often
doubled with P-90 guitars. Jazzmaster/Divided by 13 supported some clean sounds.
Malakian describes a clearer attack and higher tuning than Toxicity, and a room
technique involving acoustic-guitar bodies. [D2] These are session findings,
not a confirmed Soldier Side track sheet. Use British Hot-Rod for the main
heavy sound, a contrasting clear clean and an articulate sustained lead.
An ordinary room reverb does not reproduce that resonant tracking room.

## Physical tuning verification

All notes are **sixth string to first**, without a capo. Tune the instrument
physically. A song's chord key does not establish string tuning. Use a suitable
string gauge/setup for lower tunings; fret accurately and compare with the
specific release for small pitch offsets. No exact reference-frequency offset
has been measured in this research.

logs. Recheck against an authorized score and actual reference before promoting
them to recording-verified status. The app catalog makes this uncertainty visible.
There is **no spectral pitch** in this expansion. All templates fit Legacy 128;
the Creative 4096 profile is unnecessary. A tuning change is not an amp preset.

## Proposed rack roles and matching method

These choices follow the evidence but remain **application design inferences**.
Full parameter recipes, ordered processors, cabinet IDs and conservative output
levels live in `assets/sounds/library.json`; reusable voices can be applied to
individual pedals. `catalog.json` separates evidence, tuning, pickups and notes.

| Song | Independent complete sounds | What to compare first |
|---|---|---|
| Outlaws | Clear clean, broad crunch, singing lead, bowed-layer adaptation | Clean attack versus saturated body; halo is an optional picked/swelled adaptation, not a bow simulator |
| Revolution Radio | Punch rhythm, forward lead | Midrange chord bite, low-string recovery and pick attack; width needs another performance |
| September Ends | Acoustic source, electric clean adaptation, heavy rhythm, melodic lead | Preserve acoustic decay; keep heavy saturation separate from the opening; lead sustains without turning everything up |
| Outshined | Cornell-role rhythm, Thayil-role rhythm, Thayil lead | Warm core versus sharper complementary part; retain Drop-D percussiveness without modern-metal over-tightening |
| Slaves and Bulldozers | Cornell-role weight, Thayil-role bite, raw lead | Low-D bloom, riff decay and irregular articulation; do not compress away breathing or claim generated feedback |
| Heart-Shaped Box | Dark clean, abrasive rhythm, focused lead | Arpeggio note separation, asymmetric bends and dry-mid lead; decide modulation after comparing the commercial mix |
| Teen Spirit | Watery clean, explosive rhythm, melodic lead | Clean/heavy contrast; hard clipping rather than infinite amp gain; lead and rhythm stay level-controlled |
| Come as You Are | Deep mono chorus clean, chorus drive, chorus lead | Dark low-string riff; pronounced slow pitch movement and near-even wet/dry balance; compare mono before introducing width |
| Lithium | Dry clean, dark sustain fuzz | Dry quiet sound versus denser fuzzy body; the fuzz/clean-combo route is not a generic modern amp preset |
| Man Who Sold the World | Acoustic source, colored electro-acoustic lead, electric adaptation | Actual source character, modest chorus/drive, Pat's separate part; avoid heavy electric-cab filtering on a miked acoustic |
| In Bloom | Dry clean, full hard rhythm, rough lead | Spacious dynamic contrast without obligatory chorus; gritty lead articulation rather than a polished stadium wash |
| Chop Suey! | Picked clean adaptation, tight rhythm, open heavy alternative | Acoustic/doubled intro cannot be recreated by a clean electric rack; riff stops versus more open sustained chords |
| Toxicity | Defined clean, combined heavy rhythm, articulate lead | Picking separation, low-C weight and upper-mid bite; amp blend is not double tracking |
| Soldier Side | Clear clean, British heavy rhythm, singing lead | Higher Drop-C# attack and melodic sustain; no automatic harmony generation or scene switching |

Record the same dry phrase with the intended tuning/pickup, including light and
hard attacks, full chords, palm mutes, bends and a long decay. First level-match
the processed guitar to a reference guitar passage; a full mastered band mix is
not a clean guitar stem. Adjust gain for saturation, early bass for clipping
behavior, then post-cab EQ for weight/bite. Adjust chorus rate/depth/mix separately.
Only then adjust ambience and output. Do not widen an acoustic or crank distortion
to imitate drums, bass or another guitar. Existing synthetic audits establish
finite output, loading and callback heap behavior, not album realism or perceived
loudness. Physical audio/latency/deadline tests remain pending.
